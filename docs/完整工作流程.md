# 完整 Assignment 工作流程

## 1. 收集当前作业要求

只使用当前作业对应的 Assignment Brief、Rubric/Marking Scheme、Lecture Notes、老师公告和补充指示。确认题目动词、交付物、章节、字数、引用格式、来源限制、截止日期和 AI 使用规定。缺失或冲突时先列出并确认。

历史 Assignment 只能用于学习工作方法、结构和经验。不得自动复用旧作业文字、参考文献、公司数据、Rubric、截止日期或 AI 规定。旧作业自称核实过来源，不等于独立核实。考虑旧参考文献时必须重新核对来源真实性、当前相关性和对当前论点的支持。

## 2. 将 Rubric 转化为计划

逐项拆分评分标准，映射到课程概念、要证明的论点、证据来源和文章章节。讲义与某项无关时标记“不适用”，不要强行加内容。

| Rubric 项 | 课程概念/讲义位置 | 需要证明的论点 | 证据及来源 | 文章章节/段落 | 待确认问题 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 填入本次评分项 | 概念与讲义页码/章节；不适用时注明 | 明确可回答的问题 | 可追溯链接与证据位置 | 目标段落 | 缺口、冲突、限制 |

## 3. 检索并核对来源

根据具体论点设计少量精确查询，遵守老师规定的资料来源限制。优先使用学校许可的学术数据库、出版方/期刊页面、官方报告、政府或监管来源。记录查询日期、检索平台、来源链接和阅读范围。

对每篇核心来源分别追踪三项状态：

- **来源存在性：**优先核对出版方、期刊或机构正式页面；有 DOI 时核对 DOI 对应记录。一个元数据服务无结果不证明来源不存在。
- **书目信息准确性：**分别核对标题、完整作者名单及顺序、期刊/出版物、年份和 DOI；保留核查来源链接。不一致时标记“待确认”。
- **论点支持：**将来源对应到具体论点，尽量阅读全文，记录页码、段落、表格或数据位置。只有摘要或元数据时明确标记，不能据此声称全文支持论点。

三项状态不能互相替代，也不能证明期刊质量或同行评审状态。不要把 AI 生成的引用直接当成真实来源。

## 4. 整理证据

起草前记录每项核心来源的主要观点、具体证据、支持的论点、拟放章节、阅读状态、适用范围、局限和不同观点。若证据不支持原论点，应调整论点或另找来源，不能扭曲来源。

对公司或定量数据记录：

- 数据名称和数值；
- 报告名称、报告年度和来源链接；
- 页码、表格或章节；
- 单位、币种、统计期间；
- 计算公式、输入值和结果（如适用）；
- 是否独立核实及限制。

找不到原始来源或准确位置时标记“待确认”，不得补造。

## 5. 分段写作

先确认用户负责或请求的章节，不重写已完成部分。按本次 Brief、Rubric、Lecture Notes 和字数要求组织段落。每段有清楚主张，解释课程概念和证据如何支持主张，并回应题目。区分事实、推断、建议和个人判断；不把相关性直接写成因果关系，不夸大研究结论。

## 6. 引用与语言修改

默认引用格式应根据使用者所在学校当前官方指南确定；TAR UMT 学生可先核对学校图书馆当前 APA 指南。老师的本次作业要求优先，任何不同规则单独记录。检查文内引用与参考文献表互相对应，并核对字段和格式。格式检查或 DOI 元数据匹配不能证明论文内容支持论点。

使用清晰、自然、正式的本科生英文；润色须保持原意、证据强度、限定语和引文。

## 7. 对照 Rubric 最终审核

逐项记录达成、部分达成或缺失，附文章位置、证据和待补事项。检查回答题目、要求覆盖、字数结构、来源限制、论证、数据、引用、格式、英文、图表/附录和提交规范。尚未核实的内容明确列出，不声称全部通过。

## 8. 交付报告

说明完成范围、主要论点及证据、来源核查状态、阅读范围、Rubric 检查情况、未解决事项和用户需要人工完成的步骤。

## English

### 1. Collect Current Assignment Requirements

Use only the current Assignment Brief, Rubric/Marking Scheme, Lecture Notes, instructor announcements, and supplementary instructions. Confirm the task verbs, deliverables, sections, word count, citation style, source limits, deadline, and AI-use rules. List missing or conflicting requirements and clarify them first.

Past assignments may be used only to learn methods, structure, and experience. Do not automatically reuse old wording, references, company data, rubrics, deadlines, or AI-use rules. A past assignment's claim that a source was verified is not independent verification. Recheck a past reference for authenticity, relevance to the current task, and support for the current claim before reuse.

### 2. Turn the Rubric into a Plan

Map each criterion to course concepts, claims to demonstrate, evidence sources, and sections. Mark a lecture concept “not applicable” when it does not fit; do not force it into the answer.

| Rubric criterion | Course concept/lecture location | Claim to demonstrate | Evidence and source | Section/paragraph | Open question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enter this assignment's criterion | Concept and lecture page/section; state N/A when appropriate | A question the section must answer | Traceable link and evidence location | Planned paragraph | Gap, conflict, limitation |

### 3. Find and Verify Sources

Build a small set of precise searches around specific claims and follow the instructor's source restrictions. Prefer institution-authorized databases, publisher/journal pages, official reports, and government or regulatory sources. Record the search date, platform, source link, and reading scope.

Track three separate statuses for each core source:

- **Source existence:** Prefer the official publisher, journal, or institutional page. If there is a DOI, check its record. No result from one metadata service does not prove that the source does not exist.
- **Bibliographic accuracy:** Check the title, complete author list and order, journal/publication, year, and DOI separately; retain the verification links. Mark discrepancies “to be confirmed.”
- **Claim support:** Map the source to a specific claim, read the full text where possible, and record page, paragraph, table, or data locations. Label abstract-only or metadata-only review; do not claim that it supports the full-text argument based on that alone.

These statuses are not interchangeable and do not establish journal quality or peer-review status. Do not treat an AI-generated citation as a real source without verification.

### 4. Organize Evidence

Before drafting, record each core source's main point, specific evidence, supported claim, planned section, reading status, scope, limitations, and differing views. If the evidence does not support the proposed claim, revise the claim or find another source; do not distort the source.

For company or quantitative data, record:

- data name and value;
- report title, report year, and source link;
- page, table, or section;
- units, currency, and period;
- formula, inputs, and result if calculated;
- whether independently verified and any limitations.

If the primary source or exact location is unavailable, mark it “to be confirmed”; do not invent it.

### 5. Draft in Stages

Confirm which section the user is responsible for or has requested. Do not rewrite completed sections. Follow the current Brief, Rubric, Lecture Notes, and word count. Each paragraph should state a clear claim, explain how concepts and evidence support it, and answer the task. Distinguish facts, inferences, recommendations, and personal judgments. Do not turn correlation into causation or overstate findings.

### 6. Citations and Language

Determine the default citation style from the user's current institutional guide; TAR UMT students may begin with the current library APA guide. The instructor's requirements for the current assignment take precedence, and any different rule should be recorded separately. Check that in-text citations and the reference list correspond, and verify their fields and format. Formatting checks or DOI metadata matches do not prove that a paper supports a claim.

Use clear, natural, formal undergraduate English. Editing must preserve meaning, evidence strength, qualifications, and citations.

### 7. Final Rubric Review

Record each criterion as met, partially met, or missing, with its location, evidence, and remaining action. Check the task response, coverage, word count, structure, source limits, reasoning, data, citations, format, English, charts/appendices, and submission rules. List unverified items and do not claim full compliance without checking.

### 8. Handover Report

Report the completed scope, main claims and evidence, source-check statuses, reading scope, rubric review, unresolved issues, and steps the user must complete manually.
